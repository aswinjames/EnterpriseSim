## 1. Summary

This RFC defines how a reflection's outputs actually change future behavior: the
**`LearningEvent`** as the single, typed, durable record of every change to memory or policy,
and the **propagation protocol** that routes those events to the Experience Layer, the Knowledge
promotion queue, and the retrieval/planning/routing **policy stores** consumed by the Context
Layer and Decision Intelligence. It also defines **loop closure** — the measurement that a
lesson demonstrably altered a later execution — which per `ADR-0034` is the only definition of
"learning" the ECL accepts.

## 2. Motivation

`ARCH-05` is explicit: *a lesson that never changes future behavior is not learning.* Reflection
(`RFC-0016`) produces lessons and proposed policy updates, but those are inert until propagated,
applied, and confirmed. The organization needs one auditable ledger of what changed, why, and
whether it worked — otherwise improvement is asserted, not proven, and the continuous-improvement
flywheel (CANON-001 §9 Stage 8) cannot be measured. The `LearningEvent` and its propagation
protocol are that ledger and that guarantee.

## 3. Guide-level explanation

The `sdk.learning.LearningEngine` consumes each `ReflectionObject` and emits one or more
`LearningEvent`s (`sdk.objects.LearningEvent`, `schemas/learning_event.schema.json`). Each event
has a `kind`:

- `experience_written` — a new/refined `EXP-###` (handled by `sdk.experience.ExperienceStore`).
- `promotion_proposed` — an `EXP-###` crosses the corroboration bar for Knowledge promotion
  (handed to `RFC-0018`).
- `policy_updated` — a retrieval/planning/routing policy change is applied.
- `pattern_detected` — a systemic, cross-execution pattern is raised to the Architecture Office.

Events flow onto the Signals Bus (`RFC-0028`) and to their targets. A `policy_updated` event
mutates a versioned policy record that the Context Layer (`BudgetPolicy`, retrieval templates)
or Decision Intelligence (`Planner` heuristics, `ModelRouter` hints) reads on the *next* task.
When a subsequent execution's reflection confirms the lesson changed the outcome, the originating
reflection's `loop_closed_by` is set and the `LearningEvent.loop_closed` flips to `true`.

## 4. Reference-level explanation

### 4.1 The `LearningEvent` contract

Per schema, each event carries `kind`, `source_reflection` (`REF-###`), `target` (the
`KN-###`/`EXP-###`/policy ID affected), an open `payload`, and `loop_closed` (bool). Events are
**append-only and immutable**; a correction is a new event, never an edit — consistent with the
never-reuse, additive-and-reversible properties of `ARCH-05` and `ADR-0022`.

### 4.2 Policy stores as versioned records

Policies are durable, versioned, and canonically identified so they can be referenced,
diffed, and rolled back. Three policy families exist:

| Family | Consumer interface | Example directive |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval policy | `sdk.context.ContextAssembler` / `Retriever` (`RFC-0002`) | "for `APP-003` feature changes, raise priority of `APP-015` domain rules" |
| Planning heuristic | `sdk.planner.Planner` (`RFC-0008`) | "feature-change plans must include negative-side-effect assertions" |
| Routing hint | `sdk.models.ModelRouter` (`RFC-0010`) | "Tier 0 subtle-side-effect tasks → high-reasoning class" |

A `policy_updated` event references the prior policy version and the new one; the active version
is what the next task reads. Because policies are data, not code, applying a lesson requires **no
redeploy** and is fully reversible (`ARCH-05` Best Practice 7).

### 4.3 Loop closure (the definition of learning)

Per `ADR-0034`, a lesson is *learned* only when a later execution measurably reflects it. The
protocol:

1. A `policy_updated`/`experience_written` event is emitted with `loop_closed = false`.
2. On a later task, the Context Layer injects the `EXP-###` or the retrieval policy fires; the
   `PlanningObject.applied_experience` or provenance records the application.
3. That task's reflection (`RFC-0016`) detects the applied lesson and a non-regressed outcome and
   sets `loop_closed_by`.
4. The Learning Engine flips the originating event's `loop_closed` to `true` and emits the
   **loop-closure signal** consumed by `RFC-0028` and the improvement-trend metric.

Lessons whose loop never closes within a configurable window are surfaced as **open loops** — a
first-class failure mode (`ARCH-05`) prompting policy review.

### 4.4 Idempotency & conflict resolution

Propagation is idempotent on `(source_reflection, kind, target)`: re-processing a reflection
does not duplicate events. When two reflections propose contradictory policy updates to the same
target, the Learning Engine records both events and defers to the higher-corroboration lesson;
unresolved contradictions raise a `pattern_detected` event for human governance rather than
silently overwriting (mirrors Knowledge contradiction handling in `ARCH-02`).

### 4.5 Systemic pattern detection

Beyond per-task events, the Learning Engine aggregates `root_cause.category` across many
`REF-###`. When a threshold of same-category failures recurs (e.g., three promotion-window cache
incidents), it emits `pattern_detected` targeting the Architecture Office (`TEAM-090`) with the
supporting `REF-###`/`INC-####` set — an organization-level lesson, not a per-task one (`ARCH-05`
Example C).

## 5. Drawbacks

- **Policy sprawl.** Many narrow retrieval/planning tweaks can accumulate and interact
  unpredictably; mitigated by versioning, periodic consolidation, and measuring policy-improvement
  lift (`ARCH-05`).
- **Delayed confirmation.** Loop closure can lag by many tasks, so near-term improvement is not
  immediately visible; the open-loop signal bounds the wait.
- **Attribution ambiguity.** When several policies change between two executions, crediting the
  improvement to one lesson is imperfect; addressed by A/B-style policy trials in future work.

## 6. Rationale & alternatives

- **Direct writes from the Reflector** (rejected): scatters mutation across the codebase and
  loses the single auditable ledger; the `LearningEvent` centralizes and types every change.
- **Fine-tuning the model on outcomes** (rejected): violates the model-agnostic invariant
  (`ARCH-07`); improvement must be data, portable across providers.
- **Closing the loop by assertion** (rejected): counting "lessons written" rewards volume, not
  improvement; `ADR-0034` insists on measured closure.

## 7. Prior art

Event-sourcing (append-only event logs as the source of truth), feature-flag/configuration
management (versioned, rollback-able policy without redeploy), and control-theory feedback loops
(measure the effect of a change, not just the change) are the generic patterns. CloudEvents-style
typed events (`CANON-001` §8 principle 9) inform the envelope shape. No proprietary system.

## 8. Unresolved questions

- What is the right open-loop window per task type before a lesson is deemed ineffective?
- Should routing hints be governed more strictly than retrieval policies, given their cost impact
  (`RFC-0029`)?
- How to attribute improvement cleanly when multiple policy changes overlap — controlled trials
  vs. observational inference?

## 9. Future possibilities

- **Automated policy search** — trial retrieval/planning variants offline and keep those with
  proven `EVAL-###` lift (`ARCH-05` future evolution), all as data.
- **Cross-Worker curriculum** — sequence propagated lessons so a new Worker type warm-starts from
  another's accumulated policies (`RFC-0020`).
- **Policy provenance graphs** — visualize which reflections produced which active policies and
  their measured lift, on the Signals Bus (`RFC-0028`).
