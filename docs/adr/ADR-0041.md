# ADR-0041: Policy Enforcement Before Execution

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-05-05
- Deciders: Architecture Office (TEAM-090)
- Related: ARCH-06, ADR-0008, ADR-0040, ADR-0042, ADR-0045, RFC-0030

## Context

A Worker's guardrails are only as good as the moment they are checked. If policy is evaluated
*after* a tool acts — after a PR is merged, a payment captured, a record deleted — the guardrail
is a post-mortem, not a control. ARCH-06 names **guardrail bypass** (a high-risk change
proceeding without required approval) as a failure mode and states the mitigation plainly:
policy is enforced *before* execution, and CANON-001 §7 approvals are hard gates, not
suggestions. The SDK reflects this with a `PolicyGuard` in the decision module, distinct from
the tools that execute.

MCG's non-negotiables — required approvals by change class, PCI cardholder-data boundaries,
segregation of duties, risk tiers (CANON-001 §4.6, §7.5) — must be impossible for a Worker to
route around, whatever its confidence.

## Decision

We will enforce policy **before** any execution directive reaches a tool, per RFC-0030.
Decision Intelligence (ADR-0008) evaluates the applicable policy set against the intended action
and blocks it if any hard gate fails — independent of, and prior to, confidence-based control
(ADR-0040):

- Hard gates include: required approvals for the change class/tier (CANON-001 §7.5), PCI/auth
  boundary rules, segregation of duties, and least-privilege tool scope (ADR-0042).
- A policy block stops the action regardless of how confident the Worker is; policy is not a
  confidence input, it is a veto (ARCH-06 confidence table: "Policy-blocked → stop regardless").
- Every policy evaluation and activation is written to the decision trace (ADR-0021) and emitted
  as a guardrail-activation signal (ADR-0038).
- Policies are one of the four Worker specialization edges (ADR-0025); the enforcement mechanism
  is shared and identical across Worker types, only the policy set differs (PCI vs.
  bias/fairness vs. data-minimization).

## Consequences

- Positive: guardrails cannot be bypassed — unsafe actions are stopped before they touch MCG
  systems; auditable proof of every gate evaluated.
- Positive: idempotency and rollback requirements (ADR-0045) are checked as pre-execution
  policy, so an action that cannot be safely undone is blocked before it runs.
- Negative: the policy engine must be complete and correct — a missing policy is a silent gap,
  and an over-broad policy blocks legitimate work; policies need the same review rigor as
  Knowledge.
- Negative: pre-execution evaluation adds a step to every action's latency.
- Neutral: policy sets evolve; changes to policy that alter an ECL invariant require an ADR
  (ADR-0050), while routine policy tuning is a governed configuration change fed by Learning
  Engine signals.
