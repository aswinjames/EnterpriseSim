# ADR-0017: Separate Outcome Confidence From Judgment Confidence

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-03-02
- Deciders: Architecture Office (TEAM-090)
- Related: ARCH-04, ADR-0013, ADR-0015, ADR-0016

## Context

"How confident are we?" is ambiguous in evaluation, and the ambiguity is dangerous. There are
two very different questions hiding in one word:

1. *How good is the work?* — the score itself (did it pass the gates and rubric?).
2. *How certain are we about that score?* — the reliability of the judgment producing it.

These can diverge sharply. A deterministic gate can be *certain* that coverage is 84% (high
judgment confidence) about work that is only *mediocre* (moderate outcome). A model-assisted
"is this well-designed?" item can express a *high* opinion of the work while the *judgment
itself* is unreliable. If the two are collapsed into a single "confidence" number, Decision
Intelligence and the Learning Engine cannot tell a solid pass from an uncertain guess, and
fused confidence (`ADR-0013`) mixes incomparable things.

## Decision

We will require the Evaluation Layer to report **two distinct confidences that must never be
conflated**: **outcome confidence** (how good the work is, tied to the score) and **judgment
confidence** (how certain the evaluation is about that score). Objective, deterministic gates
(`ADR-0015`) yield high judgment confidence; model-assisted qualitative items yield lower
judgment confidence and always carry linked evidence (`ADR-0016`). When judgment confidence is
low, the verdict is marked *provisional* and may be routed to human review. Evaluation never
launders a low-confidence guess as a high-confidence score.

## Consequences

- **Positive.** Fused confidence in DI (`ADR-0013`) combines like with like — it can weight a
  high-outcome/low-judgment item cautiously and act boldly only on high-outcome/high-judgment
  results. Autonomy gating (`ADR-0014`) becomes meaningfully safer.
- **Positive.** The Learning Engine can discount lessons grounded in low-judgment-confidence
  evaluations, preventing "feedback poisoning" where shaky judgments become confident lessons.
- **Negative.** Producers and consumers of evaluations must carry and reason about two numbers
  instead of one, and rubric authors must estimate judgment confidence per item — more modeling
  effort.
- **Neutral.** The separation also enables calibrating model-assisted judges against human
  ground truth over time, re-weighting judgment confidence as their reliability is measured.
