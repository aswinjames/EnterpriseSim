# ADR-0035: Root-Cause Reflection Over Symptom Fixing

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-03-24
- Deciders: Architecture Office (TEAM-090)
- Related: ARCH-05, ARCH-01, ADR-0034, ADR-0020, ADR-0021

## Context

Reflection (CANON-001 §9, Stage 6) converts outcomes into lessons. Its value depends entirely
on finding the *true* origin of an outcome, not the surface error. ARCH-05 names two failure
modes directly: **hindsight bias** (rationalizing the outcome) and **symptom fixation** (fixing
the surface error so the failure recurs). The canonical example (ARCH-05, Example A):
guest checkout created orphaned Loyalty accounts because context `CTX-0091` *dropped* the
loyalty domain rules for budget and the plan lacked a negative assertion — the root cause was
missing experience plus a context-selection policy, not the specific line of code.

A lesson that fixes the symptom guarantees a recurrence. The most common real cause is a
context item that was available but excluded — which is only discoverable if reflection inspects
the dropped-candidate log (ARCH-01).

## Decision

We will require reflection to diagnose **root cause**, not symptom, and to ground every causal
claim in evidence:

- Each `ReflectionObject` records a root-cause `category` and `detail` with a root-cause
  confidence, classified to a true origin: missing knowledge, dropped context, flawed plan,
  tool error, or evaluation gap (ARCH-05).
- Reflection must inspect **what context was dropped** (ARCH-01 provenance), not only what was
  used — dropped-but-relevant items are the most common cause.
- Every claim is grounded in Evaluation evidence (ADR-0020 provenance, ADR-0016 no score
  without evidence) and the decision trace (ADR-0021); a diffuse failure with no single clear
  cause yields a *hypothesis* lesson flagged for corroboration, not a confident conclusion.
- Fixes derived from a reflection target the cause: they update retrieval/planning policies
  (Context, Decision Intelligence) so the class of failure cannot recur, not just the instance.

## Consequences

- Positive: prevents recurrence; root-cause accuracy and regression-prevention become
  measurable signals (ARCH-05) and feed loop-closure (ADR-0034).
- Positive: the discipline of checking dropped context tightens the Context Layer's retrieval
  policies over time — the organization gets structurally better at assembling context.
- Negative: root-cause reflection is slower and more evidence-hungry than patching the symptom;
  it depends on complete provenance and decision traces being present.
- Negative: over-diagnosis risk — attributing a coincidental failure to a deep cause; mitigated
  by root-cause confidence and the hypothesis-lesson mechanism.
- Neutral: some root causes are organizational (a recurring systemic pattern across incidents);
  these escalate to the Architecture Office as systemic-pattern reports (ARCH-05, Example C).
