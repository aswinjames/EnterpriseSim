# ADR-0001: Record Architecture Decisions

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-01-05
- Deciders: Architecture Office (TEAM-090)
- Related: CANON-001 §4.7, ARCH-07, ADR-0022, ADR-0050

## Context

The Enterprise Cognitive Layer (ECL) is a permanent architecture (`ARCH-01`…`ARCH-07`)
carrying many load-bearing invariants: the model is stateless, learning lives outside the
weights, knowledge is immutable once published, autonomy is confidence-gated. These
invariants are only as durable as the memory of *why* they exist. `CANON-001` §4.7 already
mandates that "significant decisions are recorded as ADRs," but mandate without a format and
a home produces inconsistent, unfindable rationale.

Without a disciplined record, decisions rot in three predictable ways: they get silently
re-litigated by new contributors, they get quietly violated because nobody remembers the
trade-off they encoded, and they get lost entirely as people rotate. EnterpriseSim is a
*public benchmark*; the reasoning behind its design must itself be inspectable and
reproducible — exactly the property it demands of the artifacts its Workers produce.

## Decision

We will record every architecturally significant decision as an **Architecture Decision
Record** in the strict Michael Nygard format — **Context / Decision / Consequences** — one
decision per file at `docs/adr/ADR-000N.md`, zero-padded to four digits, numbered globally
and never reused (`ADR-0022`). ADRs are authored and owned by the Architecture Office
(`TEAM-090`).

An ADR, once **Accepted**, is immutable. A decision is never edited in place; it is changed
only by a *new* ADR that supersedes it, preserving the full decision history. Every ADR
cites the `CANON-`, `ARCH-`, `RFC-` and prior `ADR-` IDs it depends on, so the decision graph
is navigable. ADRs record *decisions and trade-offs*; detailed design remains in the `ARCH-`
documents and `RFC-`s, which ADRs reference rather than duplicate.

## Consequences

- **Positive.** Design intent becomes durable, greppable and onboarding-friendly. Benchmark
  reviewers and future Worker authors can audit *why* the ECL is shaped as it is, not just
  *what* it is. The supersession chain yields a decision history instead of a mutable, lossy
  document.
- **Positive.** A common format makes decisions comparable and lowers the cost of reviewing a
  proposed change against prior reasoning.
- **Negative.** Writing ADRs is friction; trivially small decisions must be filtered out or
  the catalog dilutes and reviewers stop reading it. Significance is a judgment call the
  Architecture Office must exercise.
- **Neutral.** ADRs are prose, not code or schema. Where a decision changes a contract, an
  `RFC-` accompanies it; where it changes an invariant, canon supremacy (`ADR-0050`) applies.
