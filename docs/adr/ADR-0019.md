# ADR-0019: Hybrid Retrieval (Semantic + Keyword + Graph)

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-03-09
- Deciders: Architecture Office (TEAM-090)
- Related: ARCH-02, ARCH-01, ARCH-03, ADR-0007, ADR-0023

## Context

Retrieval quality is the ceiling on Worker quality: because learning lives outside the model
(`ADR-0003`), a fact that is not *retrieved* into context cannot help, no matter how good the
model is. A single retrieval mode leaves predictable blind spots. Pure semantic (vector)
search finds meaning but misses exact tokens — it can fail to surface the doc that literally
names `APP-012`. Pure keyword search finds exact terms but misses paraphrase and concept. Both
miss *relationships* — "what breaks if `APP-007` changes?" is a graph question neither can
answer. "Retrieval blindness," where relevant knowledge exists but is never found, is a leading
root cause of Worker failure.

We must decide the retrieval strategy for Knowledge (`ARCH-02`) and Experience (`ARCH-03`).

## Decision

We will use **hybrid retrieval** combining three complementary modes, and take the union of
their results as candidates for ranking:

- **Semantic (vector)** — matches meaning and paraphrase against task intent.
- **Keyword / lexical** — catches exact terms and canonical IDs (`APP-###`, `KN-###`, error
  strings) that embeddings blur.
- **Graph** — traverses typed relationships between apps, teams, services, rules and incidents
  (depends-on, causes, supersedes), enabling multi-hop retrieval.

Candidates from all three are merged, then ranked for relevance, authority and freshness by the
Context Layer (`ARCH-01`) within the model budget. This applies to both governed Knowledge and
situational Experience retrieval.

## Consequences

- **Positive.** The union recovers results no single mode finds — the canonical example: a
  "PCI cardholder data boundary" query where semantic surfaces general security docs, keyword
  catches `APP-012`, and graph adds the related `SEC` threat model. Coverage improves and
  context starvation drops.
- **Positive.** Graph retrieval directly exploits the referential integrity the whole repo
  maintains (`ADR-0023`), turning canonical IDs into navigable relationships.
- **Negative.** Three indexes (vector, lexical, graph) cost more to build, keep fresh, and
  merge; result fusion needs a ranking policy, and the graph must be maintained as relationships
  change. More moving parts, more failure surface.
- **Neutral.** The three modes can be tuned independently and their fusion weights learned over
  time against which retrievals led to high `EVAL-###` scores (confidence-calibrated retrieval).
