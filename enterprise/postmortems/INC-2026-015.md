## Summary

REL-2026-008 introduced a regression in the returns-eligibility logic of the Customer Portal
(APP-021, My Meridian). A boundary condition in the updated eligibility rule caused some
**delivered orders that were in fact returnable** to be shown as **ineligible** for
self-service return. For ~30 minutes, a subset of NA customers attempting to start a return
were incorrectly told their order could not be returned online. No returns were wrongly
approved; the error was over-restrictive. It was resolved by reverting the eligibility rule
change. Customers blocked during the window could still return via support channels, limiting
harm.

## Impact

- **Users:** A subset of NA customers saw eligible orders incorrectly marked ineligible for
  self-service return for ~30 minutes; they were blocked from the self-service flow but not
  from returning through support.
- **Regions:** NA only.
- **Revenue-proxy:** Minimal direct revenue impact (returns are cost/experience, not sales);
  estimated ~$4K in support-deflection and experience cost. Reputational/CSAT cost from a
  frustrating returns experience is the more relevant harm.
- **Data integrity:** No incorrect returns were approved; no order or refund data corrupted.

## Root cause

**Category: code_regression.** The returns-eligibility rule updated in REL-2026-008 contained
a boundary-condition error (an off-by-one / inclusive-vs-exclusive comparison on the delivery
date or return window) that classified some genuinely returnable orders as ineligible. The
regression was not caught because the test suite lacked cases at the eligibility-window
boundaries. It is a straightforward code regression exposed by real order data spanning the
boundary.

## Detection

Automated plus support signal. The returns-funnel monitor detected the anomalous rate of
"ineligible" outcomes, corroborated by support contacts. Detection was reasonably fast for a
Sev3; the gap was test coverage of boundary conditions, not runtime monitoring.

## Resolution

The eligibility rule change was reverted, restoring correct eligibility determinations.
Customers who had been blocked during the window and contacted support were assisted directly.
A corrected rule with explicit boundary tests was scoped as follow-up before re-attempting the
change.

## What went well

- The returns-funnel monitor surfaced a subtle logic regression via its effect on outcome
  distribution, not just errors.
- The failure was over-restrictive (no wrongful approvals), and the support channel provided
  a fallback so no customer was truly unable to return.
- Revert was quick and low-risk.

## What went wrong

- The eligibility rule change shipped without tests at the eligibility-window boundaries.
- Boundary conditions (inclusive vs. exclusive comparisons on dates/windows) are a known
  regression hotspot that was not specifically guarded.

## Lessons learned

- Boundary conditions on dates and windows are a perennial regression source; they must be
  tested explicitly, not incidentally.
- Monitoring outcome distributions (not just error rates) catches logic regressions that
  don't throw errors.
- A working fallback channel (support-assisted returns) meaningfully reduces the severity of
  a self-service regression — design for graceful fallback.
