## Summary

Following REL-2026-002, the Search & Browse service (APP-006) briefly displayed stale prices
in search results. A change in the release altered catalog-to-index propagation timing such
that price updates from the Product Catalog (APP-005) took longer than usual to reach the
OpenSearch index backing search. For roughly 13 minutes, some search result cards showed a
previous price while the product detail page and cart (which resolve price live from Pricing,
APP-007) showed the correct current price. The discrepancy was cosmetic on the search surface
only — no customer was charged an incorrect amount, because authoritative price resolution
happens downstream at cart/checkout, not in the search index. The reindex pipeline caught up
and the incident resolved.

## Impact

- **Users:** NA shoppers browsing search results saw stale price labels on a subset of items
  for ~13 minutes. Prices corrected on click-through to the product page.
- **Regions:** NA only.
- **Revenue-proxy:** Negligible; estimated <$3K exposure from potential confusion-driven
  bounce. No mischarges — authoritative price is resolved live at cart/checkout.
- **Data integrity:** No corruption. The index was temporarily stale, not wrong; it
  converged once propagation caught up.

## Root cause

**Category: data_issue.** REL-2026-002 adjusted the batching/refresh cadence of the
catalog-to-index propagation job. The new cadence increased end-to-end propagation latency
for price fields under the overnight update volume, causing the search index to briefly serve
stale prices. This was a data-freshness (propagation lag) issue, not incorrect data: the
source of truth (Catalog/Pricing) was always correct, and the index converged once the
pipeline caught up. Because authoritative price resolution occurs at cart/checkout, the
customer-facing correctness guarantee was never at risk — only the display on the search
surface was temporarily stale.

## Detection

Automated. The APP-006 index freshness monitor (which compares indexed price against the
Catalog source) detected the skew within ~5 minutes of the release and opened a Sev3. The
freshness SLO tooling worked as intended.

## Resolution

No code change was required during the incident — the reindex pipeline drained its backlog
and the index converged naturally within the propagation window. TEAM-003 verified freshness
returned to baseline. Follow-up work to right-size the propagation cadence was tracked as an
action item, since the resolution during the incident was self-healing.

## What went well

- The freshness monitor detected a subtle, non-erroring data-staleness condition quickly.
- Correct architectural layering saved the day: authoritative price lives at cart/checkout
  (APP-007), so stale search labels could not cause a mischarge.
- Cross-team confirmation (TEAM-003 + TEAM-005) rapidly isolated the lag to the index rather
  than the source data.

## What went wrong

- A release changed propagation cadence without a freshness-impact assessment against
  overnight update volume, so the added latency was unanticipated.
- There is no automated guard that blocks a search result from showing a price whose index
  age exceeds a freshness bound.

## Lessons learned

- Changes to data-propagation cadence are functional changes and deserve a freshness-impact
  assessment, especially against peak-volume windows.
- Correct layering of authoritative reads (price resolved at cart/checkout, not from the
  search index) is a powerful blast-radius limiter; preserve that invariant.
- Staleness is not the same as incorrectness — monitoring should distinguish "converging"
  from "wrong" to keep severity calibrated.
