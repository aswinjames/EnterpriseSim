## Summary

During a flash sale on 2026-03-18, the Promotions Engine (APP-008) again served **stale
cached promo data**, producing brief cart-total mismatches at checkout (APP-003) — a
recurrence of the pattern first seen in **INC-2026-007**. The targeted invalidation hook
added after INC-2026-007 covered the previously-observed price-change path but did not cover
the flash-sale activation path: rapid promotion activation/deactivation under high load left
the promo cache momentarily inconsistent with current pricing. The impact was smaller and
shorter than INC-2026-007 (Sev2, NA only) because the earlier monitor caught it fast, but the
recurrence confirmed that the tactical fix had not addressed the underlying caching design.

## Impact

- **Users:** NA shoppers checking out during the flash sale saw brief incorrect totals on a
  subset of promoted items for portions of the ~39 minute window.
- **Regions:** NA only.
- **Revenue-proxy:** Estimated ~$40K exposure from mis-totaled flash-sale transactions;
  affected orders flagged for reconciliation.
- **Data integrity:** Orders recorded totals as computed; affected orders enumerated for
  finance reconciliation and customer make-good where over-charged.

## Root cause

**Category: cache_staleness.** Same underlying class as INC-2026-007: the APP-008 promo cache
can serve data inconsistent with current pricing. The targeted invalidation added after
INC-2026-007 covered the specific price-change path but **not** the flash-sale
activation/deactivation path, where rapid promotion state changes under high load raced ahead
of cache invalidation. The tactical, path-by-path approach to invalidation was the deeper
cause — each new path that could change effective price/discount reintroduced the staleness
risk.

## Detection

Automated, via the same checkout total-consistency monitor from INC-2026-007. Detection was
fast; because responders recognized the signature immediately, correlation to the prior
incident was near-instant. This is a case where prior experience (INC-2026-007) materially
sped diagnosis.

## Resolution

A promo-cache flush restored correctness, identical in shape to the INC-2026-007 mitigation.
Affected flash-sale orders were enumerated for reconciliation. Critically, the resolution
included a decision to stop patching individual paths and to pursue a **systemic write-through
cache fix** — which was ultimately forced by the third occurrence (INC-2026-018).

## What went well

- Prior experience made diagnosis near-instant; responders recognized the signature within
  minutes.
- The mitigation was known, safe, and fast.
- The monitor from INC-2026-007 proved its value on the recurrence.

## What went wrong

- The problem recurred: the INC-2026-007 fix was path-specific and did not generalize.
- Rapid promotion activation/deactivation under load was not tested against cache
  invalidation before the flash sale.
- The organization had not yet escalated the recurring pattern into a systemic-fix mandate,
  allowing a second money-impacting incident.

## Lessons learned

- Path-by-path patching of a caching defect is a losing strategy; a second incident of the
  same class is a signal to fix the design, not the path.
- Prior experience dramatically shortens diagnosis — the value of a well-documented previous
  incident (INC-2026-007) was realized here directly.
- Two occurrences of a Tier 0 money-correctness defect should trigger a systemic-pattern
  review; waiting for a third (INC-2026-018) was too slow. This recurrence is the middle
  data point in ARCH-05 Example C.
