## Summary

During a scheduled pre-peak load test on 2026-05-03, the Storefront Web origin (APP-001)
showed significant latency degradation as synthetic load ramped, with the API Gateway
(APP-024) passing the full load through to a saturating origin. Storefront p95/p99 latency
climbed and a fraction of real NA user requests overlapping the test window were slowed. This
was a **capacity** finding surfaced by the load test — exactly what the exercise is designed
to do — but the real-traffic overlap made it a customer-affecting Sev2. It was mitigated by
tuning edge load-shedding on APP-024 to protect the origin, after which latency recovered. The
load test then continued against the better-protected configuration.

## Impact

- **Users:** A fraction of NA storefront users during the ~25 minute window experienced
  elevated page latency; no errors or data loss, but a degraded browsing experience.
- **Regions:** NA only.
- **Revenue-proxy:** Estimated ~$22K exposure from slowed sessions and potential bounce
  during the window; because there were no hard failures, most sessions completed slowly
  rather than failing.
- **Data integrity:** None affected — this was a latency/capacity event, not a correctness
  event.

## Root cause

**Category: capacity.** The Storefront origin (APP-001) did not have sufficient capacity /
autoscaling headroom for the load profile the test applied, and the edge (APP-024) was not
configured to shed or throttle excess load to protect the origin. Under ramp, the origin
saturated and latency climbed. The load test was the trigger, but the underlying condition —
insufficient origin protection at the edge ahead of peak — is a real capacity/resilience gap
that would have surfaced during an actual peak event. Overlapping the test with live traffic
made it customer-affecting.

## Detection

Automated. The APP-001 latency SLO alert fired as p95/p99 breached during ramp. Detection was
immediate. The notable process point is that the load test overlapped enough real traffic to
cause customer impact, which argues for tighter isolation or off-peak scheduling of such
tests.

## Resolution

Edge load-shedding and concurrency limits on APP-024 were tuned to protect the origin,
immediately relieving saturation and restoring storefront latency. Origin autoscaling headroom
review was scoped as durable follow-up. The load test then continued against the improved
configuration, achieving its purpose of validating peak readiness.

## What went well

- The load test did its job: it surfaced a real capacity/resilience gap before actual peak.
- Detection was immediate and cross-team response was fast.
- Edge load-shedding provided a fast, effective mitigation lever.

## What went wrong

- The load test overlapped live NA traffic closely enough to cause real customer impact;
  isolation/scheduling could have prevented that.
- Origin autoscaling headroom was insufficient for the target load profile.
- Edge load-shedding to protect the origin was not pre-configured ahead of the test.

## Lessons learned

- Load tests should surface capacity gaps without harming real users; isolate or schedule
  them to avoid live-traffic overlap.
- Edge load-shedding is a first-class resilience control; origins should be protected by the
  edge before peak, not tuned reactively during an incident.
- A "successful" incident: the exercise found a genuine gap early — the improvement is to
  capture that value without the customer-facing side effect.
