## Summary

At 04:00 UTC on 2026-06-09, a brief outage of the Enterprise Data Platform (APP-020) stalled
the Product Catalog's (APP-005) offer-ingestion pipeline, which consumes feeds from the data
platform. For ~14 minutes, new and updated offer data was not ingested, so catalog offer
information was momentarily stale (not wrong) across NA, EU, and APAC. Because Catalog serves
previously-ingested data from its own store, the storefront and search continued to function
on last-known-good offer data; only freshness of new offer updates was affected. When the
data platform recovered, ingestion caught up automatically and the backlog drained.

## Impact

- **Users:** Minimal direct impact — shoppers across NA, EU and APAC saw last-known-good
  offer data; newly changed offers were delayed by up to ~14 minutes. No errors or outages on
  consumer surfaces.
- **Regions:** NA, EU, APAC (all regions consuming the affected feed).
- **Revenue-proxy:** Negligible — estimated <$5K exposure from delayed offer updates; no
  consumer-facing failure and no mischarges (price is resolved authoritatively downstream).
- **Data integrity:** No corruption. Ingestion is designed to resume and catch up; the
  backlog drained cleanly after recovery with no lost or duplicated offer records.

## Root cause

**Category: dependency_failure.** The root cause was an upstream dependency outage: the
Enterprise Data Platform (APP-020), which supplies offer feeds to the Product Catalog
(APP-005), was briefly unavailable. Catalog's ingestion pipeline correctly stalled (it cannot
ingest what is not available) rather than failing or corrupting data. Catalog's design to
serve from its own already-ingested store, and to resume ingestion on recovery, contained the
impact to freshness only. There was no Catalog defect; the incident is a study in graceful
degradation on an upstream failure.

## Detection

Automated. The Catalog ingestion-lag monitor fired promptly when feed consumption stalled.
Detection was healthy. Cross-team confirmation with Data Platform (TEAM-060) quickly
attributed the cause upstream.

## Resolution

No action was required within Catalog beyond confirming graceful degradation and monitoring
recovery — the resolution was the recovery of the upstream data platform (owned by TEAM-060),
after which Catalog ingestion resumed and the backlog drained automatically. Follow-up focused
on making the freshness impact of an APP-020 outage explicit and bounded.

## What went well

- Catalog degraded gracefully: it served last-known-good data and consumer surfaces stayed up.
- Ingestion resumed and caught up automatically on recovery, with no data loss or duplication.
- The ingestion-lag monitor detected the stall quickly and cross-team attribution was fast.

## What went wrong

- There was no explicit, communicated freshness SLO / expectation for how stale catalog offer
  data may become during an APP-020 outage.
- Dependence on APP-020 availability for offer freshness is a single-point-in-time coupling
  that is not surfaced to downstream consumers during an outage.

| 2 | Add a downstream-visible freshness indicator/telemetry so consumers know when offer data is degraded. | Catalog eng | TEAM-005 |
| 3 | Review APP-020 outage runbook coordination between TEAM-005 and TEAM-060 for feed dependencies. | Catalog + Data Platform | TEAM-005 / TEAM-060 |
| 4 | Confirm ingestion backlog-drain behavior scales for longer APP-020 outages (load test the catch-up). | Catalog on-call | TEAM-005 |

## Lessons learned

- Graceful degradation works: serving last-known-good data and resuming ingestion on recovery
  turned an upstream outage into a minor freshness event rather than a consumer outage.
- Freshness expectations under dependency failure should be explicit SLOs, so severity and
  customer communication are calibrated.
- Cross-team fault attribution (Catalog vs. Data Platform) is fast when ownership and monitors
  are clear — a benefit of the "you build it, you run it" model.
