## Summary

For the third time in a quarter, the Promotions Engine (APP-008) served **stale cached promo
data**, causing incorrect cart totals at checkout (APP-003) — following INC-2026-007 and
INC-2026-011. This occurrence spanned NA and EU and lasted ~81 minutes, longer than the prior
two because the response deliberately went beyond the known cache-flush mitigation to
implement and validate a **systemic write-through cache fix** rather than patch yet another
path. The Architecture Office (TEAM-090) was pulled in, the recurring pattern was formally
recognized, and the incident closed with a durable fix plus a standing pre-release check for
promotion-window cache invalidation.

## Impact

- **Users:** Shoppers in NA and EU reaching checkout during the affected window saw and could
  be charged incorrect totals on promoted items; the longer duration widened the affected
  population versus the prior two incidents.
- **Regions:** NA and EU.
- **Revenue-proxy:** Sev1-level; estimated ~$160K in mis-totaled transactions across the
  window (over- and under-charges), all enumerated for reconciliation and customer make-good.
- **Data integrity:** Orders recorded totals as computed; a full reconciliation across the
  window was performed given the third occurrence and its duration.

## Root cause

**Category: cache_staleness.** The recurring root cause across all three incidents is that the
APP-008 promo cache could serve data inconsistent with current pricing, because invalidation
was TTL-based and then patched path-by-path (after INC-2026-007 and INC-2026-011) rather than
made structurally correct. The true, systemic cause is the **absence of write-through /
event-driven cache coherence** between the Pricing Service (APP-007) and the Promotions Engine
cache: whenever effective price/discount inputs changed, the cache was not guaranteed to
reflect the change synchronously. Every new path that could change effective price
reintroduced staleness — which is exactly why tactical fixes failed twice.

The Learning Engine had flagged this as a systemic pattern across INC-2026-007, -011 and -018
(ARCH-05 Example C); this incident is where the organization acted on it.

## Detection

Automated, via the same checkout total-consistency monitor used in INC-2026-007 and
INC-2026-011. Detection was immediate and diagnosis was instant given two priors. The extended
duration was a *deliberate* investment in a permanent fix, not a detection or diagnosis
failure.

## Resolution

Interim: a promo-cache flush restored correct totals within minutes (as before). Durable:
APP-008 was changed to a **write-through / event-driven invalidation** model so that price
changes in APP-007 synchronously update or invalidate the promo cache across all paths,
eliminating the class of defect rather than a single path. A **standing pre-release check**
for promotion-window cache invalidation was added to the release checklist. The fix shipped
through the fast-tracked hotfix pipeline with 2 reviews (Tier 0) and smoke + security gates,
and forward-merged to `main`. Affected orders across the window were reconciled and customers
made whole where over-charged. These learnings feed **KN-063** (promo-cache write-through
guidance).

## What went well

- Diagnosis was instant thanks to two prior incidents and a shared monitor.
- The team resisted the easy path (flush and close) and invested in a systemic fix.
- The Architecture Office was engaged appropriately to own the cross-team, organization-level
  change.
- The fix was validated against synthetic promotion-window changes before closing.

## What went wrong

- It took three Sev1/Sev2 money-impacting incidents to drive the systemic fix; the pattern
  should have been escalated after the second (INC-2026-011).
- Path-by-path invalidation was, in hindsight, an anti-pattern for a correctness-critical
  cache.
- Cumulative customer and reconciliation cost across the trio was significant and largely
  avoidable had write-through been adopted earlier.

## Lessons learned

- Correctness-critical caches require write-through or event-driven coherence, not TTL expiry
  and not path-by-path patches — this is now codified in KN-063.
- Recurrence is the signal: a repeating incident class is an organizational-design problem,
  and the trigger for systemic review should be the second occurrence, not the third.
- This trio is the canonical Learning-Engine systemic-pattern example (ARCH-05 Example C):
  many executions aggregated into one organization-level lesson, a new KN runbook, and a
  standing pre-release check — improvement at the level of the organization, not just the task.
