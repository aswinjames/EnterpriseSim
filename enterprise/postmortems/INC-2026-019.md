## Summary

REL-2026-010 introduced a **synchronous** call to the Promotions Engine (APP-008) on the
Checkout Service (APP-003) critical path. Under load, this added a blocking dependency to the
order-placement flow and regressed checkout p99 latency. The regression was caught within
minutes by the checkout latency SLO and resolved quickly by making the promotions call
asynchronous (non-blocking) on the checkout path, restoring p99 to baseline. No orders failed;
the impact was slower checkout for a subset of NA sessions during the brief window.

## Impact

- **Users:** A subset of NA shoppers experienced slower checkout completion for ~8 minutes;
  no failures, just elevated latency at the tail.
- **Regions:** NA only.
- **Revenue-proxy:** Very small — estimated ~$5K exposure from tail-latency-induced friction;
  the short duration and absence of hard failures kept this minimal.
- **Data integrity:** None affected; a latency-only event.

## Root cause

**Category: capacity.** A new synchronous dependency was placed on the latency-sensitive
checkout critical path. Adding a blocking call means checkout p99 now includes the promotions
call's tail latency; under load this pushed checkout past its latency SLO. The service was not
short of raw capacity in isolation, but the critical-path latency budget was exceeded by the
added synchronous hop — a capacity/latency-budget regression. Checkout critical-path changes
should default to asynchronous or budgeted calls; a synchronous addition was the defect.

## Detection

Automated. The checkout p99 latency SLO alert fired at onset and the change was fresh in
REL-2026-010, making correlation near-instant. Detection and diagnosis were both fast,
yielding the 8-minute resolution.

## Resolution

The promotions call was made asynchronous / non-blocking on the checkout path (fronted by a
flag), removing it from the synchronous latency budget and restoring p99 to baseline. A
durable design guideline — no unbudgeted synchronous calls on the checkout critical path — was
reinforced as follow-up.

## What went well

- Fast detection and near-instant correlation to a fresh release gave an 8-minute resolution.
- The fix was a safe, reversible flag/config change, not a risky redeploy.
- No orders failed — the regression was latency-only.

## What went wrong

- A synchronous call was added to the latency-sensitive checkout critical path without a
  latency-budget review.
- Performance testing before release did not flag the added critical-path latency under load.

## Lessons learned

- The checkout critical path has a strict latency budget; any synchronous addition spends
  that budget and must be justified or made asynchronous.
- Tier 0 latency-sensitive paths need a performance gate that reflects realistic load, not
  just functional correctness.
- Feature-flagging the call made the fix instant and reversible — flags on new critical-path
  dependencies are cheap insurance.
