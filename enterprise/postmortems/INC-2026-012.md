## Summary

Following REL-2026-006, a reservation race in the Inventory Service (APP-010) briefly allowed
**oversell** on high-demand SKUs stocked across multiple warehouses. For a ~24 minute window,
concurrent available-to-promise (ATP) reservations against the same multi-warehouse SKU could
both succeed against overlapping stock, so Order Management (APP-009) accepted slightly more
orders than there was physical inventory to fulfill. A small number of orders were placed
that could not be fully fulfilled from the intended warehouses. The race was closed by
tightening the reservation path to serialize decrements across warehouse partitions.

## Impact

- **Users:** A small number of NA customers placed orders on affected high-demand SKUs that
  later required re-sourcing from an alternate warehouse or, in a few cases, backorder/cancel
  handling.
- **Regions:** NA only.
- **Revenue-proxy:** Modest — estimated ~$30K in orders requiring re-sourcing or
  cancellation; most were re-sourced without customer impact. Reputational cost from any
  cancellations exceeds the direct revenue figure.
- **Data integrity:** Inventory counts were briefly over-committed; reconciled against
  physical stock after the fix. Affected orders were individually triaged by OMS.

## Root cause

**Category: data_issue.** REL-2026-006 introduced a reservation path in APP-010 that did not
correctly serialize decrements for SKUs stocked across multiple warehouses. Two concurrent
reservations could each read the same available quantity and both succeed, decrementing past
zero — a classic read-modify-write race across warehouse partitions. The available-to-promise
data was momentarily inconsistent with physical reality, and OMS (APP-009) trusted the
over-optimistic ATP and accepted the orders. It is a data-consistency defect in the
reservation semantics, exposed under concurrent high-demand load.

## Detection

Automated. The inventory-consistency monitor detected over-committed/negative ATP within
minutes of the condition arising. Detection was healthy; the gap was the absence of a
concurrency test for the multi-warehouse reservation path before release.

## Resolution

The reservation path was constrained to serialize decrements for affected multi-warehouse
SKUs (per-SKU guarded decrement), closing the race. ATP reconciled against physical stock.
The small set of over-committed orders was triaged by OMS — most re-sourced from alternate
warehouses, a few handled via backorder/cancel. A durable fix hardening reservation
concurrency semantics was scoped as follow-up.

## What went well

- The inventory-consistency monitor caught the over-commit quickly, before it grew large.
- Cross-team response (Inventory + OMS) was fast, containing order-level fallout.
- The over-commit was small in absolute terms; most affected orders were re-sourced without
  customer-visible impact.

## What went wrong

- A change to reservation semantics shipped without a concurrency/race test for the
  multi-warehouse path.
- Reservation decrements across warehouse partitions were not serialized, allowing a
  read-modify-write race under concurrent load.

## Lessons learned

- Reservation/decrement logic is inherently concurrent and must be tested under concurrency;
  single-threaded tests will pass while the race lurks.
- ATP is a Tier 0 correctness signal that OMS trusts implicitly — over-optimistic inventory
  data converts directly into unfulfillable orders.
- Changes to consistency-critical semantics (reservations, decrements) warrant race testing
  and atomic operations as a default, not an afterthought.
