## Summary

During a promotion window activated as part of REL-2026-004, a pricing change did not take
effect at checkout because the Promotions Engine (APP-008) served **stale cached promo data**.
When the underlying price changed in the Pricing Service (APP-007), the promotions cache in
APP-008 was not invalidated, so the Promotions Engine continued to compute discounts against
the old price. The result was **incorrect cart totals at checkout** (APP-003) — the customer
saw a total that did not reflect the current, correct price/discount combination. This was a
Sev1 because it directly corrupted the money shown and charged at checkout across two regions.
It was mitigated by forcing a promo-cache flush and confirming totals recomputed correctly.

## Impact

- **Users:** Shoppers in NA and EU reaching checkout during the promotion window saw and
  could be charged incorrect totals for ~45 minutes on items whose price changed within the
  window.
- **Regions:** NA and EU.
- **Revenue-proxy:** Sev1-level exposure; estimated ~$120K in mis-totaled transactions
  across the window (a mix of over- and under-charges), all flagged for reconciliation and
  customer make-good where charged high.
- **Data integrity:** Orders recorded the totals as computed; affected orders were
  enumerated for finance reconciliation and customer remediation.

## Root cause

**Category: cache_staleness.** The Promotions Engine (APP-008) caches promo/price inputs for
performance. When a price changed in APP-007 during an active promotion window, no
invalidation propagated to the APP-008 cache, so the engine kept computing discounts against
the stale price. Checkout (APP-003) faithfully presented the resulting (incorrect) total. The
cache had no write-through or event-driven invalidation tied to upstream price changes — it
relied on TTL expiry, which is far too slow for a live promotion window.

This is the **first observed instance** of a pattern that would recur (INC-2026-011,
INC-2026-018): *promotion-window price changes do not flush the APP-008 cache.* At this stage
the fix applied was tactical (flush + a targeted invalidation on the known path).

## Detection

Automated. The checkout total-consistency monitor detected the mismatch within minutes and
the incident was escalated to Sev1 given the direct money impact. Customer reports arrived in
parallel and corroborated. Detection was healthy; prevention was the gap.

## Resolution

Immediate mitigation was a forced flush of the APP-008 promo cache, after which the engine
repopulated from current APP-007 pricing and checkout totals recomputed correctly. A targeted
invalidation hook for the specific price-change path was scoped as the near-term fix.
Affected orders were enumerated for finance reconciliation and customer make-good. No schema
change was required.

## What went well

- The checkout total-consistency monitor caught a money-impacting defect quickly.
- Three owning teams converged fast on a shared bridge and correlated across the
  price→promo→checkout chain.
- A safe, reversible mitigation (cache flush) restored correctness without a risky deploy.

## What went wrong

- The APP-008 cache had no event-driven invalidation tied to upstream price changes; it
  relied on TTL expiry, unsuitable for live promotion windows.
- Promotion-window activation and price changes were not coupled to a cache-invalidation
  step in the release process.
- The tactical fix addressed the known path but did not generalize — leaving the door open
  for recurrence (which did occur in INC-2026-011).

## Lessons learned

- TTL-based caches are unsafe for data that must be correct within a live promotion window;
  correctness-critical caches need write-through or event-driven invalidation.
- A tactical, path-specific fix to a caching defect invites recurrence; the systemic pattern
  should have been recognized earlier (it took three incidents — see INC-2026-018 and
  ARCH-05 Example C).
- Money shown at checkout is Tier 0 correctness; any change that can move a live price must
  be coupled to cache invalidation. This lesson feeds KN-063 (promo-cache write-through
  guidance) and the eventual systemic fix.
